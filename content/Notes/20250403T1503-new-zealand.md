# New Zealand

1840: first colonial establishment in Wellington, capital of New Zealand
1907: Became a self-governing Commonwealth nation
1947: Became an independent country

Often called kiwis (from the kiwi bird)

Indigenous peoples: Māori
Northern island called "Te Ika a Māui" ("The fish of Māui")
Southern island: "Te Waka a Māui" ("The canoe of Māui")
Small island south of that: "Te Punga o Te Waka a Māui" ("The anchor stone of Māui's canoe")
Māui is a demigod, half human for the Māori

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Māori_mythology
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Māui_(Māori_mythology)

New Zealand called "Aotearoa" by the Maori
Ancestors of Maori arrived around 1200 AD.
Around 15% of the population
Te Reo Maori is their native language, one of the NZ official languages
"Kia ora": hello
"Haere mai": welcome

Europeans arrived in NZ in early 1800s
Initial relations were friendly
Treaty of Waitangi signed by representatives of Britain's Queen Victoria, various Maori chiefs
NZ became part of the British Empire

Maori lost land through confiscation to British settlers
Land is still a significant issue

Maori has many traditions, passed on through generations
Haka
Chanted Maori poems with expressive movements.
Ha: Breath
Ka: Fire
Haka is a dance
War dance
Do haka before war
To frighten and intimidate the opponents
Famously done before rugby matches by the NZ Rugby Team - All Blacks

Hongi - greeting where noses are pressed together
Tattoos (ta moko) to decorate their bodies
Important part of their culture, expression of commitment and respect
Men wear moko on face, buttocks, thighs and arms
Women wear moko on the chin and lips

Tradition lately became more integrated in the New Zealand society.
Language is one of the official languages since 1985
Many places have Maori names

Seven seats of total 120 are reserved for Maori in NZ parliament
Estimated 620 000 Maori, 15% of the population
