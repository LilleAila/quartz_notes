### Tasks

- In lines 34–38, p. 220, the reader is taken back to Lahore (flashback). What do we learn about Parvez and his relationship to religion?
  This flashback shows that Parvez has had previous bad experiences with religion, which has led him to be opposed to religion for the rest of his life.

- When Parvez tells his colleagues that Ali is not a drug addict but that he has discovered religion, his colleagues “became oddly silent” (line 45, p. 220). Why?
  At first, the colleagues are talking about how it's bad to use drugs, as they think he did, and that he should not do it. When they learn that he has discovered religion, they turn silent, because they don't want to be opposed to religion, and it changed how the viewed Ali from being a drug addict to just being a regular person, but with religion.

- What does Parvez answer when his son tells him that it is wrong to drink alcohol and relish pork pies? Why does Parvez want to drink?
  Parvez sees drinking as a reward, after all his hard work. On the other hand, Ali tells him that it is wrong to drink alcohol and to drink pork, because his new religion states that it is wrong do do such things. Thus, he wants his father to stop doing it, and get him to pray with him, because otherwise he will not achieve paradise.

- Parvez wants his son to respect him. To what extent does Parvez respect his own wife?
  Parvez does not respect his wife a lot. Firstly, he does not talk to his wife about the events that are happening with his son. In addition, he does not treat her well, for example when "He ordered her to sit down and keep quiet, though she had neither stood up nor said a word".

- At the restaurant, Parvez and the readers are introduced to Ali’s ideas about the UK. Comment on some of Ali’s quotes:
  “You are too implicated in Western civilization”(line 16, p. 222).

  Parvez responds "Implicated!", "But we live here!". He is surprised at the thought of being too implicated in Western civilization.

  “The Western materialists hate us”(line 3, p. 223).
  “My people have taken enough. If the persecution doesn’t stop, there will be jihad . I, and millions of others, will gladly give our lives for the cause … For us, the reward will be in Paradise”(lines 14–18, p. 223).
  How does Parvez respond?

- Parvez asks his son why he talks like this. What does Ali answer?
